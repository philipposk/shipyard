# The voice: takes, languages, quotas and pacing

Read this after the script is approved (step 3 of the skill).

- **Pick the key lines by ear.** The AI voice now and then says a word wrong, and the speech recogniser that makes the word timings does not notice: it once heard a garbled Greek word as correct, with full confidence. For the lines that matter (the name reveal, the end card, every line in another language), make a few fresh takes: `video-voice.mjs <appDir> --script <id> --takes 4 --lines reveal,end` (a few cents, no timeline). They land in `video/out/takes/<script>/`. Give the student the files to listen to (send them if your tool can) before any render. For a line that follows other sound (a recorded clip, a quote), mix that sound and the music at its lowered level into the sample, with the same gap the video will have: whether the start sounds weak or rushed can only be heard in context. Set `"take": <number>` on the line they pick and run the voice step again.
- **Fix one line without touching the rest**, in the script: `say` (what the voice reads, for example a name spelled the way it should sound), `style` (a different delivery for that line), `pauseCap` (a shorter pause), `leadIn` (keeps a soft first sound). `references/script.md` has the details. To remove one long pause from a take the student likes, cut it out of that take by hand: ffmpeg `atrim` on both sides of the pause, joined with a 10 ms `acrossfade`, keeping about 40 ms before a stop sound such as k, p or t. Save it next to the original as the next take (`video/public/voice/_raw/<voice>/<id>-<hash>.t<k>.wav`) and set `"take": <k>`. Re-voicing usually loses the delivery that made the take good. Never paste a word in from another take: it sounds like an echo. Make more takes instead.
- **Another language:** `--lang <code>` (word timings in that language, for example `el`), a `--style "<how to speak>"` written in that language, and `--wpm` low enough that nothing is sped up (Greek: `--wpm 100`). A 1.2x speed-up audibly warped Greek words. Give `--lang` to `video-publish.mjs` too.
- **Gemini:** its free tier has tight daily voice quotas (about 10 requests a day per model; the exact numbers are per project in AI Studio and vary). An error 429 that names `FreeTier` means the key's Google project has no billing set up: tell the student before planning re-takes.
- **ElevenLabs:** `--provider eleven --voice <voice id> [--speed 0.9]` with `ELEVENLABS_API_KEY` in the keys file. Its free plan is not for commercial videos (the paid Starter plan is). There is no style prompt; voices that worked: Sarah `EXAVITQu4vr4xnSDxMaL` (warm, confident) and Brian `nPczCjzI2devNBz1zQrb` at `--speed 0.9` (deep, dry). Some stock voices read too fast. A real user chose these over an OpenAI voice with a "calm, confident" style, which sounded slow.
- **Pacing:** people hear "slow and odd" at once. "Calm" or "confident" styles made lines drag. Write the script without needless commas: a comma in the middle of a short phrase makes a pause ("ten free projects every month" reads better without one). But once the student likes a take that has one long pause, do not drop the comma and voice it again (in Greek, takes without the comma sounded foreign): cut the pause out of that take (next point). If a line sounds off, try a natural-pace style or another voice, and send the student two short samples to pick from.
- **People talking inside the video** (a client on a call, a team): write their lines in a spec file and run `video-voice.mjs <appDir> --clips video/capture/specs/<name>.json`. Each line gets its own voice (pick clearly different ones), and the word times let a practice page type the words as they are spoken. `references/footage.md` has the format.
