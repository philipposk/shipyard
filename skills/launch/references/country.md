# Country checklist

Rules for selling online depend on where the student lives and where their customers are. You research it; you do not decide it. Everything here is "not legal or tax advice, confirm with an accountant".

## How to research

Use only official sources. Search the web for the student's country plus the topic and open only:
- the national tax authority
- the business register or chamber that registers businesses
- the consumer protection authority
- the data protection authority (list of EU ones: edpb.europa.eu)
- EU portals for EU countries: Your Europe (europa.eu/youreurope), the EU One Stop Shop (OSS) for VAT

Do not rely on blogs, forums or AI summaries. Quote nothing long; link the page. If sources disagree or a page is old, say so.

## What to find

1. **Do I need to register as a business?** Many countries allow small freelance or sole-trader income with simple registration; some require it before the first sale.
2. **Sales tax or VAT.** Do they need to register, and from what income? In the EU, selling digital services to consumers in other EU countries above 10,000 euro a year in total means VAT of the buyer's country, handled through the OSS. All other thresholds: write "check current value" and link the source.
3. **Invoicing.** What an invoice or receipt must contain. Stripe can send receipts; say whether that is enough in their country.
4. **Consumer rights.** Withdrawal period for digital subscriptions (14 days in the EU and UK), refund rules, required information before purchase.
5. **Privacy rules.** Which authority, whether registration is needed, what the privacy page must include.
6. **Payouts.** How Stripe pays out in their country and which bank details Stripe asks for.
7. **A merchant of record** can take over VAT and invoicing for a fee: see `payments.md`. Mention it if the tax work looks heavy.

## Write `LAUNCH-CHECKLIST.md`

In the app folder. One short section per topic above: what applies, the step the student must take, the source link, and the date you looked. Put at the top: "This is research from official sources, not legal or tax advice. Confirm with an accountant before you take real payments." Mark each item Todo / Done. The student ticks them off; you update it when they report.

## Then

Offer: "Want me to find an accountant checklist, or move on to payments?" Do not recommend a specific accountant or lawyer.
